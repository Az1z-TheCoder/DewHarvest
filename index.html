5. My condensate sample

Model and limits

Water quantity. Mass balance on water vapour across the cooling coil: condensate = dry-air mass flow × (Woutdoor − Wcoil), where W is the humidity ratio 0.622·e/(P − e) and the air leaves the coil saturated. Saturation vapour pressure from the Magnus equation (Alduchov and Eskridge 1996). Only moisture brought in with fresh air is counted; moisture from people and infiltration is not.

Carbonate chemistry. Henry's law for CO₂, the two dissociation steps of carbonic acid, water autoionisation and a charge balance, solved for pH by bisection. Equilibrium constants and calcite solubility as functions of temperature from Plummer and Busenberg (1982). Treatment: CO₂ is added, then CaCO₃ dissolves in a closed contactor (each mole adds one mole of calcium, one of carbon and two equivalents of alkalinity) until [Ca²⁺][CO₃²⁻] = Ksp. The CO₂ dose is solved so that the dissolved calcite equals the hardness target.

Disinfection. HOCl ⇌ H⁺ + OCl⁻ with pKa from Morris (1966).

Limits. Activity coefficients are taken as 1 (reasonable below about 2 mmol/L ionic strength). Copper, zinc, ammonia, dust and microbes are not modelled, and real contactors stop slightly short of equilibrium. The default hardness target and desalination energy are editable assumptions. This is a design-comparison tool. It does not certify water as safe to drink.
